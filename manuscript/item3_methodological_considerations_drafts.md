# Item 3 — "Methodological considerations" subsection: two drafts for comparison

Context: KF26 (Discussion roadmap sentence) promises a standalone "Methodological
considerations and future directions" subsection; KF32 asks to fold/delete it.
These conflict and need one decision. The subsection currently (line 278–279)
makes two points: (1) tissue preservation/DNA extraction yields, and (2) the
need for manipulative experiments on host genotype/traits to test causality
for FEF assembly. Note that the Conclusions (line 289) already restates both
points in general terms ("improved tissue preservation and sequencing
workflows will sharpen inference on core versus transient taxa"; "common-garden
and reciprocal-transplant designs that jointly manipulate host genotype, leaf
traits, and environment can test causal links among filters") — so there is
real redundancy already in the current draft regardless of which option is
picked.

No new citations or claims are introduced in either draft below; both reuse
citations already present in the manuscript.

---

## Option A — Keep standalone, reframe positively

**Roadmap sentence (Discussion intro, line 240):** unchanged — it already
promises this subsection.

**Revised subsection text** (replaces line 278–279, same heading):

> ## *Methodological considerations and future directions*
>
> Beyond the ecological patterns above, several methodological choices in
> this study point to concrete next steps rather than limitations alone.
> Preserving leaf tissue fresh or in liquid nitrogen, rather than in CTAB
> solution held at room temperature, would likely improve DNA extraction
> yields and could reveal additional taxa currently missed by our pipeline.
> Because host trait filtering and geographic structuring both left
> detectable signatures in the FEF community, the logical next step is
> manipulative work: common-garden and reciprocal-transplant experiments
> that vary host genotype and leaf functional traits directly, rather than
> inferring their effects from natural variation, would let us test whether
> trait differences causally shape FEF colonization. Such experiments would
> also let us ask whether the genetic loci underlying leaf lobing in *M.
> laciniatus* [@ferris2015; @tataru2023] independently predict fungal
> community composition, and whether taxa such as *V. victoriae* contribute
> functionally to host performance in cold, high-elevation environments.

Pros: minimal structural disruption; gives the methodological point its own
visible home; easy to point reviewers to.
Cons: doesn't resolve the redundancy with Conclusions (line 289) — both
sections would independently call for common-garden/manipulative work and
better tissue preservation.

## Option B — Fold into main text, delete the standalone subsection

**Roadmap sentence (Discussion intro, line 240):** drop the trailing clause.

> Below, we first discuss inter- and intraspecific patterns in leaf
> functional traits along elevation, then interpret elevational trends in
> FEF alpha and beta diversity, followed by trait/elevation effects on
> community composition and dispersion, geographic distance–decay patterns
> within species, and finally the consistent dominant taxa observed across
> sites.

**Delete** the "## *Methodological considerations and future directions*"
heading and its paragraph entirely.

**Fold point 2 (manipulative experiments / genotype–FEF link) into the
existing common-garden paragraph**, line 251 (end of the leaf-traits
Discussion section — this paragraph already makes almost the same point
about leaf traits, so this just extends it to FEF communities):

> Disentangling environmental and genetic drivers of leaf trait variation
> will require various experimental approaches, particularly common gardens
> (as in Ferris and Willis [-@ferris2018]) to fully answer questions about
> the functional significance of leaf traits for local adaptation to
> different elevations. Experimental manipulation with *Mimulus* is
> relatively easy due to its short generation time, high fecundity,
> self-compatibility, and ease of greenhouse propagation [@society2019;
> @wu2008]. The same tractability makes it possible to extend these designs
> beyond leaf traits: manipulating host genotype and functional traits
> directly, rather than inferring their effects from natural variation,
> would let future work test whether host genetic loci shape FEF community
> assembly and how these communities in turn affect host performance under
> elevational stress. Because these traits can act as host filters on
> microbial colonization, we next consider whether FEF diversity shows
> parallel elevational patterns within species.

**Fold point 1 (tissue preservation) into the "Consistent taxa" section**,
line 276 (appended to the end of that paragraph, where core-vs-rare taxon
detection is already being discussed):

> ...The presence of *V. victoriae* in our samples might be indicative of
> its potential role in the local adaptation of *Mimulus* to cold and high
> elevation environments. Future surveys using fresh or rapidly frozen
> tissue rather than room-temperature CTAB preservation may improve DNA
> extraction yields and taxon recovery, sharpening inference on which taxa
> are truly core versus under-detected in our sampling.

Pros: removes the KF26/KF32 conflict outright; each point lands next to the
result it's most relevant to, which is more persuasive than a generic
limitations list; addresses the "needs more positive framing" note by
turning both points into forward-looking extensions of specific findings
rather than a separate caveats section.
Cons: more structural surgery (three edit sites instead of one); the added
sentence in the "Consistent taxa" section is close in substance to the
Conclusions sentence about tissue preservation — worth deciding whether to
keep both or trim the Conclusions sentence for redundancy once this is
picked.
